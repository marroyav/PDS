\section{Observable and response conventions}

\subsection{Define the observable before adding backgrounds}

Let $R_{\gamma\to\mathrm{PE},j}$ be the time-averaged rate of
\emph{primary} photoelectrons caused by scintillation photons in optical
unit $j$, with complete light integration and linear response. For the VD
comparison, $j$ denotes one whole XA, summed over its readout channels:
\begin{equation}
R_{\gamma\to\mathrm{PE},j}
=R_{39,j}+R_{\mu,j}+R_{\mathrm{other\,rad},j},
\qquad [R]=\mathrm{PE/s}.
\label{eq:components}
\end{equation}
For the ND, specify whether $j$ denotes a panel, channel, or sum of panels.
A per-XA rate cannot be compared directly with a per-TPC rate.

Measured charge in single-photoelectron units also contains primary dark
avalanches and correlated avalanches (crosstalk and afterpulsing). A simple
linear response model is
\begin{equation}
R_{Q,j}\simeq M_j\bigl(R_{\gamma\to\mathrm{PE},j}+D_j\bigr),
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
where $D_j$ is the primary dark-avalanche rate and $M_j$ is the mean measured
charge, in SPE units, per primary avalanche for the chosen integration
procedure. If light and dark pulses have different gate acceptance, use
separate response factors. Correlated noise must not be added a second time
if the adopted PE calibration or efficiency already includes it.

A PE rate is not a pulse, trigger, or data rate. Those additionally depend on
burst sizes, thresholds, pulse finding, integration windows, and dead time.

\section{From radioactive activity to detected light}
\label{sec:kernel}

Define $K_j(\bm r)$ as the expected primary PE in unit $j$ per scintillation
photon emitted isotropically at $\bm r$, averaged over the relevant emission
spectrum. It includes optical propagation and the detector response. For a
uniform decay density $a_{39}$ and spatially constant mean yield
$\overline N_{\gamma,39}$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{39,j}=a_{39}\,\overline N_{\gamma,39}
\int_{V_{\mathrm{opt}}}K_j(\bm r)\,dV .
}
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
Here $V_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is the argon volume optically visible to the unit.
It need not equal the charge-active or fiducial volume. If source density,
yield, or emission spectrum varies, keep those quantities inside the integral.

The calculation therefore follows the chain
\begin{equation*}
\underbrace{a_{39}\,dV}_{\text{decays/s}}
\quad\times\quad
\underbrace{\overline N_{\gamma,39}}_{\text{photons/decay}}
\quad\times\quad
\underbrace{K_j}_{\text{primary PE/photon}}.
\end{equation*}
For unobstructed direct light, constant device efficiency, and a planar
entrance aperture,
\begin{equation}
K_j^{\mathrm{direct}}(\bm r)
=\epsilon_j\int_{A_j}
\frac{\cos\theta(\bm r,\bm u)}{4\pi|\bm r-\bm u|^2}
\,s(\bm r,\bm u)\,dA_{\bm u},
\label{eq:directkernel}
\end{equation}
where $\bm u$ is a point on the aperture, $\theta$ is the incidence angle
between the aperture-to-source direction and its source-facing normal,
and $s$ is survival along that ray.
With $s=1$, the integral is $\Omega_j/(4\pi)$. Reflected or scattered photons
require additional paths in $K_j$; they are not generally described by a
survival probability multiplying a direct solid angle.

\section{A direct-light geometry benchmark for cathode XAs}

\subsection{Reference geometry and area convention}

Use rectangular source volumes on either side of a horizontal cathode.
$X,Y$ span the cathode and $L$ is the drift distance on \emph{one} side;
these are local model coordinates, not the experiment's axis convention.
The reference dimensions are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
 & $X$ (m) & $Y$ (m) & $L$ (m) \\
\midrule
ProtoDUNE-VD & 6.8 & 3.0 & 3.5 \\
DUNE FD-VD & 13.5 & 60.0 & 6.5 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The ProtoDUNE dimensions follow the reported $6.8\times3\times7$ m active
volume~\cite{pdvd_geometry}; the FD dimensions are the reference design
in Ref.~\cite{fdvd_geometry}. They define this benchmark, rather than a
survey of the latest as-built geometry. Argon outside these boxes is excluded.

Take $A=0.60\times0.60=\SI{0.36}{m^2}$ for \emph{one face} of an XA.
The cathode design has 320 double-sided XAs~\cite{fdvd_pde}, but that count
does not enter a per-XA calculation. Contributions from the two source
volumes will be added explicitly; do not double $A$ as well. Match the area
convention to the efficiency calibration: use either the active entrance
area and its PDE, or a footprint-averaged response that includes inactive
regions, without applying the same loss twice.

\subsection{Derivation for an XA at the center of the cathode}

In the small-aperture approximation, a source at $(x,y,z)$ on one side has
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{geom}}(x,y,z)
\simeq \frac{A}{4\pi}\frac{z}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}},
\qquad z>0.
\end{equation}
Define the geometrical effective volume $G=\int_V P_{\mathrm{geom}}\,dV$.
It has units of volume, but it is an acceptance-weighted integral, not a
region in which every decay is detected. For the centered reference geometry,
\begin{align}
\frac{G_{\mathrm{side}}}{A}
&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^L\Omega_{\mathrm{slice}}(z)\,dz,
\label{eq:Gside}\\
\Omega_{\mathrm{slice}}(z)
&=4\tan^{-1}\!\left[
\frac{ab}{z\sqrt{z^2+a^2+b^2}}\right],
\qquad a=X/2,\quad b=Y/2.
\end{align}
$\Omega_{\mathrm{slice}}$ is the solid angle of a rectangular \emph{source
slice} as seen from the aperture center. It arises after integrating the
kernel over $x,y$; it is not the XA solid angle seen by an individual source.
For two equal drift regions and equal sensitivity on both faces, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ell_{\mathrm{geom}}\equiv\frac{G_{\mathrm{DS}}}{A}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^L\Omega_{\mathrm{slice}}(z)\,dz.}
\label{eq:ell}
\end{equation}
$\ell_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is a length. Since $0\leq\Omega_{\mathrm{slice}}\leq2\pi$,
$0\leq\ell_{\mathrm{geom}}\leq L$; it approaches $L$ when the transverse source
plane becomes infinite. A larger transverse area therefore does not produce
an unlimited per-XA rate at fixed $L$. This explains why total mass is the
wrong scaling variable for this geometry comparison.

The small-aperture expression is not a valid pointwise probability very near
the XA. The exact aperture solid angle remains bounded by $2\pi$ on each
side. Averaging Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gside}, with shifted source bounds, over the
finite aperture removes this approximation. Off-center XAs likewise require
shifted bounds; the central result is not an average over installed devices.

\subsection{Numerical result and checks}

Numerical checks are provided by \path{tools/check_background_model.py}.
The integrations give:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Quantity & ProtoDUNE-VD & FD-VD \\
\midrule
$\ell_{\mathrm{geom}}$, small aperture (m) & 1.60998 & 4.68883 \\
$G_{\mathrm{DS}}=A\ell_{\mathrm{geom}}$ (m$^3$) & 0.57959 & 1.68798 \\
$\ell_{\mathrm{geom}}$, finite $0.6\times0.6$ m aperture (m) & 1.60406 & 4.68815 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus the centered small-aperture acceptance ratio is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\frac{G_{\mathrm{FD}}}{G_{\mathrm{PD}}}
=\frac{4.68883}{1.60998}=2.91235.}
\label{eq:ratio39}
\end{equation}
Finite aperture averaging gives approximately 2.923; it changes this ratio by
less than 0.4\% in the stated geometry. This small correction does not bound
uncertainties from device placement, optical transport, or source-volume choice.

\section{Normalize the radioactive contribution}

The DEAP-3600 atmospheric-argon measurement is~\cite{ar39}
\begin{equation}
S_{39}=(0.964\pm0.001_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm0.024_{\mathrm{sys}})
\ \mathrm{Bq/kg}.
\end{equation}
With the reference liquid density $\rho=\SI{1395}{kg/m^3}$,
\begin{equation}
a_{39}=S_{39}\rho=\SI{1344.78}{s^{-1}.m^{-3}}.
\end{equation}
This assumes atmospheric argon with the quoted activity; substitute the
appropriate value for a different argon inventory.

For lossless direct propagation and constant device efficiency, the result is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{R_{39,j}^{\mathrm{direct}}
=a_{39}A_j\ell_{\mathrm{geom},j}\,
\overline N_{\gamma,39}\,\epsilon_j.}
\label{eq:R39}
\end{equation}
The benchmark coefficients $a_{39}G_{\mathrm{DS}}$ are
\SI{779.4}{s^{-1}} (ProtoDUNE) and \SI{2270.0}{s^{-1}} (FD).
They are geometrically weighted decay rates, \emph{not} detected event rates.
Multiply them by photons per decay to obtain incident photons/s, and then
by PDE to obtain primary PE/s.

The mean photon yield is a beta-spectrum average,
\begin{equation}
\overline N_{\gamma,39}
=\int f_{39}(E)\,N_\gamma(E;\mathcal E,c_{\mathrm{Xe}})\,dE,
\qquad \int f_{39}(E)\,dE=1,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E$ is the electric field and $c_{\mathrm{Xe}}$ the xenon
concentration. Use the energy actually deposited in the optically modeled
volume. Do not use the beta endpoint as the mean decay energy.

Device PDE is defined per photon incident on the aperture, not per photon
created in the argon. The cited XA study corrects its PE for crosstalk and
reports configuration-dependent efficiencies up to $(4.5\pm0.4)\%$ at
4.5 V overvoltage~\cite{fdvd_pde}. The front-page estimate adopts 4.5\%
as a reference response, not an established efficiency for every installed XA.
Neither the PDE nor the scintillation spectrum should be transferred between
pure and Xe-doped argon without checking their wavelength dependence.

\section{Cosmic light and what the CRT can measure}

For one muon trajectory $\Gamma$, the same response kernel gives
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{PE},\mu}^{(j)}
=\int_{\Gamma\cap V_{\mathrm{opt}}}
Y_\gamma(\ell)\left(\frac{dE}{d\ell}\right)_\mu
K_j(\bm r(\ell))\,d\ell,
\label{eq:muPE}
\end{equation}
where $Y_\gamma$ is photons per deposited energy, with local field and
ionization-density dependence. This is a track-light approximation;
showers, secondaries, and light from outside the selected track volume need
additional contributions if significant.

Define differential muon intensity $\Phi_\mu$ per area \emph{normal to the
track}, per time, solid angle, and energy. Then
\begin{equation}
R_{\mu,j}=\int dE_\mu\,d\Omega\int dA_\perp\,
\Phi_\mu(E_\mu,\Omega)\,
N_{\mathrm{PE},\mu}^{(j)}(E_\mu,\Omega,\bm r_0).
\label{eq:muRate}
\end{equation}
Integrate over incoming trajectories once. If a quoted flux is instead per
horizontal area, use its corresponding projected-area convention; do not
insert a second cosine correction. A scalar surface-to-underground flux
ratio alone does not account for changed energy and angular distributions.

\subsection{Convert tagged events into a rate}

For equal-duration on-tag and matched off-time gates, let
\begin{equation}
\Delta n_j=\langle n_{j,\mathrm{on}}\rangle
-\langle n_{j,\mathrm{off}}\rangle
\qquad [\Delta n_j]=\mathrm{PE/tag}.
\end{equation}
After matching detector conditions and correcting PE response, the
contribution associated with the \emph{accepted tag population}, within the
chosen gate, is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{R_{\mu,j}^{\mathrm{tag,gate}}
=f_{\mathrm{tag}}\,\Delta n_j,\qquad
f_{\mathrm{tag}}=N_{\mathrm{tag}}/T_{\mathrm{live}}.}
\label{eq:CRT}
\end{equation}
The product has units PE/s. The conditional excess $\Delta n_j/\Delta t$
is a rate \emph{during selected gates}, not the time-averaged cosmic rate.

To infer all-muon light, correct for tag efficiency, live time, gate light
acceptance, and the position/direction/energy selection of the tagger.
Correcting only the total tag rate is insufficient if missed muons have a
different PE yield. Use binwise corrections or a validated response model;
regions of zero tag acceptance require an independent constraint. Reject or
account for duplicate tags and overlapping gates.

An off-tag sample still contains untagged cosmic light. An off-time sample
estimates the unrelated background under a tag, including accidental muons.
Neither is automatically a pure $^{39}$Ar sample. Random triggers, gain and
dark-noise calibration, and a residual-cosmic estimate are needed to separate
the terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:components}.

\section{What can be extrapolated to the far detector?}

For the same approximations as Eq.~\eqref{eq:R39},
\begin{equation}
\frac{R_{39}^{\mathrm{FD}}}{R_{39}^{\mathrm{PD}}}
=\frac{a_{39}^{\mathrm{FD}}}{a_{39}^{\mathrm{PD}}}
\frac{A_{\mathrm{FD}}}{A_{\mathrm{PD}}}
\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{FD}}}{\ell_{\mathrm{PD}}}
\frac{\overline N_{\gamma,39}^{\mathrm{FD}}}{\overline N_{\gamma,39}^{\mathrm{PD}}}
\frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{FD}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{PD}}}.
\label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
The value 2.912 follows only when every factor except geometry cancels.
For general optical transport, replace $A\ell\epsilon$ by
$\int K_j\,dV$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:master}. Evaluate the actual positions
before forming an average over many XAs; sum their rates for an array total.

The track-only cosmic benchmark is much smaller than the $^{39}$Ar reference
in FD-VD. This does not establish the dominant component of measured charge:
other radioactivity, instrumental noise, showers, and acquisition acceptance
are separate inputs. Apply the geometry ratio
only to an independently estimated $^{39}$Ar component, not to all off-tag
charge or to the full ProtoDUNE background.

\section{Measurements needed for a prediction}

A quantitative comparison requires:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the actual XA/panel positions, active entrance areas, and optically
    visible argon volume, including any light through openings;
    \item a beta-spectrum-averaged scintillation yield at each operating
    field and xenon concentration;
    \item device PDE and optical propagation calibrated for the appropriate
    spectrum, incidence angles, and operating voltage;
    \item random-trigger light, primary dark rate, correlated-noise response,
    and acquisition acceptance in consistent PE units;
    \item CRT-tagged excess light versus track geometry and a constraint on
    missed cosmics and other radiological sources.
\end{enumerate}
The reference calculation now gives explicit per-channel light rates for
all four detectors: $^{39}$Ar contributes about $10^4$--$10^5$ PE/s/channel
in ProtoDUNE-VD, $10^5$ in FD-VD, $10^2$ in full ND-LAr, and a few $10^1$
in ND 2$\times$2. These are conditional optical estimates. The listed inputs
are needed to turn them into operating-detector predictions or trigger rates.
