\section{DUNE Near Detector \texorpdfstring{2$\times$2}{2x2} demonstrator}
\label{sec:nd2x2}

The ND-LAr 2$\times$2 demonstrator requires a different geometrical treatment
from the VD cathode system. It consists of four optically segmented modules.
Each module is approximately
\begin{equation}
D_x\simeq\SI{0.67}{m},\qquad
D_y\simeq\SI{0.67}{m},\qquad
D_z\simeq\SI{1.8}{m},
\end{equation}
and is divided by a central cathode into two optically isolated TPC volumes.
The light system uses wall-mounted ArCLight and Light Collection Module (LCM)
detectors, with roughly 30\% optical coverage per TPC. Representative quoted
photon-detection efficiencies are
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\rm ArCLight}\sim2\times10^{-3},\qquad
\epsilon_{\rm LCM}\sim6\times10^{-3}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Intrinsic \texorpdfstring{$^{39}$Ar}{39Ar} normalization}

The quoted active LAr mass of the complete 2$\times$2 is approximately
\begin{equation}
M_{2\times2}\simeq\SI{2.6}{t}.
\end{equation}
For atmospheric argon with $S_{39}=0.964$ Bq/kg,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{39,\rm decay}^{2\times2}
=S_{39}M_{2\times2}
\simeq(0.964)(2600)
\simeq\SI{2.51e3}{s^{-1}} .
}
\end{equation}
Assuming four equivalent modules and two optically isolated TPCs per module,
\begin{align}
R_{39,\rm decay}^{\rm module}
&\simeq\SI{6.27e2}{s^{-1}},\\
R_{39,\rm decay}^{\rm TPC}
&\simeq\boxed{\SI{3.13e2}{s^{-1}}}.
\end{align}
This mass-based normalization is preferable to deriving the active mass from
the external module dimensions.

\subsection{Exact wall-detector geometrical acceptance}

For optical detector $j$ mounted on a wall, the direct-light probability from
a source at $\bm r$ is
\begin{equation}
P_j(\bm r)=\frac{\Omega_j(\bm r)}{4\pi},
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_j$ is the solid angle subtended by the detector.

For a centered rectangular detector of half-widths $a,b$ at normal distance
$d$,
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\rm rect}
=
4\tan^{-1}\left[
\frac{ab}{d\sqrt{d^2+a^2+b^2}}
\right].
\end{equation}
For an off-axis source the exact rectangular solid angle is
\begin{equation}
\Omega_j(\bm r)
=
\sum_{p=\pm1}\sum_{q=\pm1}
pq\,\tan^{-1}\left[
\frac{X_pY_q}
{d\sqrt{d^2+X_p^2+Y_q^2}}
\right],
\label{eq:NDsolidangle}
\end{equation}
where $X_p,Y_q$ are signed distances from the projected source position to
the four panel edges.

For a uniform source distribution in one TPC, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal G_j^{\rm ND}
=
\frac{1}{V_{\rm TPC}}
\int_{V_{\rm TPC}}
\frac{\Omega_j(\bm r)}{4\pi}\,d^3r .
}
\label{eq:NDgeom}
\end{equation}
This is the mean direct-photon interception probability of detector $j$.

The $^{39}$Ar PE rate is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm PE,39}^{(j)}
=
a_{39}V_{\rm TPC}\,
\overline N_{\gamma,39}\,
\mathcal G_j^{\rm ND}\,
P_{{\rm transport},j}\,
\epsilon_j .
}
\label{eq:ND39exact}
\end{equation}
Once the actual ArCLight/LCM panel positions and dimensions are inserted,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NDgeom} can be evaluated channel by channel without a full
optical Monte Carlo.

\subsection{Coverage approximation}

As a zeroth-order cross-check, let
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm cov}\simeq0.30
\end{equation}
be the instrumented fraction of the enclosing optical surface. If fractions
$f_A$ and $f_L$ of that area use ArCLight and LCM,
\begin{equation}
f_A+f_L=1,
\qquad
\overline\epsilon_{\rm PDS}
=f_A\epsilon_{\rm ArCLight}+f_L\epsilon_{\rm LCM}.
\end{equation}
Under an isotropically mixed surface-flux approximation,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm PE,39}^{\rm TPC}
\sim
R_{39,\rm decay}^{\rm TPC}\,
\overline N_{\gamma,39}\,
f_{\rm cov}\,
\overline\epsilon_{\rm PDS}.
}
\label{eq:ND39coverage}
\end{equation}

For illustration only, an equal instrumented-area split gives
$\overline\epsilon_{\rm PDS}\sim0.004$. Taking
$\overline N_{\gamma,39}\sim4.4\times10^3$ photons/decay,
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm PE,39}^{\rm TPC}
\sim(313)(4.4\times10^3)(0.30)(0.004)
\simeq
\boxed{\SI{1.65e3}{PE/s/TPC}}.
\end{equation}
For eight TPCs,
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm PE,39}^{2\times2}\sim\SI{1.32e4}{PE/s}.
\end{equation}
These PE rates are only order-of-magnitude checks because reflections,
absorption, panel layout, thresholds, and the precise experimental definition
of PDE have not yet been folded in. Equation~\eqref{eq:ND39exact} is the
preferred analytical calculation.

\subsection{Cosmic-muon term}

For a muon trajectory $\Gamma$ crossing a TPC, the detected PE in optical
detector $j$ is
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm PE,\mu}^{(j)}
=
\int_{\Gamma\cap V_{\rm TPC}}
Y_\gamma
\left(\frac{dE}{dx}\right)_\mu
\frac{\Omega_j(\bm r)}{4\pi}
P_{{\rm transport},j}(\bm r)
\epsilon_j\,d\ell .
\end{equation}
The time-averaged cosmic-light rate is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm PE,\mu}^{(j)}
=
\int d\Omega\int dA_\perp\,
\Phi_\mu(\theta,\phi)\,
N_{\rm PE,\mu}^{(j)}(\theta,\phi,\bm r_0).
}
\end{equation}

Thus the channel-level background model is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm PE}^{2\times2,j}
=
R_{\rm PE,39}^{(j)}
+
R_{\rm PE,\mu}^{(j)}
+
R_{\rm PE,other\,rad}^{(j)}
+
R_{\rm SiPM}^{(j)} .
}
\end{equation}

The 2$\times$2 is useful as a control measurement: the intrinsic
$^{39}$Ar normalization is only about 313 decays/s per optically isolated
TPC, while it has high optical coverage and a substantial cosmic component.
Random-trigger and externally tagged track samples can therefore separate the
continuous radiological floor from burst-like cosmic scintillation.
