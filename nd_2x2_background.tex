\section{Apply the same model to the ND 2\texorpdfstring{$\times$}{x}2}
\label{sec:nd2x2}

\subsection{Keep mass normalization consistent with optical segmentation}

The 2$\times$2 uses four modules, each with two TPC regions separated by a
central cathode. Adopt optical isolation of those eight regions as a first
approximation. Reference descriptions quote \SI{2.6}{t} of total active
argon~\cite{nd_mass}, prototype module dimensions around
$0.7\times0.7\times1.4$ m, and about 25\% optical coverage~\cite{nd_status}.
The external dimensions are not a specification of the active source volume.

With eight equal active masses,
\begin{align}
R_{39,\mathrm{decay}}^{2\times2}
&=S_{39}M=(0.964)(2600)=\SI{2506.4}{s^{-1}},\\
R_{39,\mathrm{decay}}^{\mathrm{TPC}}
&=\frac{S_{39}M}{8}=\SI{313.3}{s^{-1}}.
\label{eq:ndactivity}
\end{align}
These are actual decay rates in the specified active mass. They are distinct
from the acceptance-weighted coefficients $a_{39}G$ in the XA benchmark.
For an active-only source model at the reference density, each TPC volume
must satisfy $\rho V_{\mathrm{TPC}}=M/8$, giving
$V_{\mathrm{TPC}}\simeq\SI{0.233}{m^3}$.

For comparison, the earlier $0.67\times0.67\times1.8$ m envelope, multiplied
by four and by the liquid density, would imply \SI{4.51}{t}. It cannot be
used as a fully active source volume while normalizing to \SI{2.6}{t}.
Use the actual active boundaries for the spatial average. If additional
argon is optically visible, include it explicitly as an additional source;
it is absent from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ndactivity}.

\subsection{Calculate wall-panel acceptance before applying PDE}

For a uniform source in one TPC, the general expression is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{R_{39,j}
=R_{39,\mathrm{decay}}^{\mathrm{TPC}}\,
\overline N_{\gamma,39}\,
\left\langle K_j\right\rangle_V,\qquad
\left\langle K_j\right\rangle_V
=\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{TPC}}}\int_{V_{\mathrm{TPC}}}K_j\,dV.}
\label{eq:ND39exact}
\end{equation}
Spatially varying transport belongs inside this integral. Factoring it out
requires an explicitly acceptance-weighted average.

For lossless direct light and constant panel PDE,
$\langle K_j\rangle_V=\epsilon_j\mathcal G_j$, where
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_j=\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{TPC}}}\int_{V_{\mathrm{TPC}}}
\frac{\Omega_j(\bm r)}{4\pi}\,dV.
\label{eq:NDgeom}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal G_j$ is dimensionless. For a rectangular panel in a plane,
use normal distance $d>0$ and signed corner coordinates relative to the
source projection: $X_-=x_{\min}-x_s$, $X_+=x_{\max}-x_s$, and similarly
$Y_-,Y_+$. Its exact direct solid angle is
\begin{equation}
\Omega_j=\sum_{p=\pm1}\sum_{q=\pm1}pq\,
\tan^{-1}\!\left[\frac{X_pY_q}
{d\sqrt{d^2+X_p^2+Y_q^2}}\right].
\label{eq:NDsolidangle}
\end{equation}
This assumes an unobstructed planar aperture. With panel half-sizes $a,b$,
the centered-source limit is
\begin{equation}
\Omega_j=4\tan^{-1}\!\left[\frac{ab}{d\sqrt{d^2+a^2+b^2}}\right].
\end{equation}
Sum $\mathcal G_j\epsilon_j$ over panels for the TPC total; do not
multiply a per-TPC result by the number of channels.

\subsection{A numerical illustration, not an optical prediction}

The NuFact 2024 summary gives representative device efficiencies
$\epsilon_A\sim0.002$ for ArCLight and $\epsilon_L\sim0.006$ for LCM,
with approximately 25\% photocoverage~\cite{nd_status}. They are illustrative
values for the stated configuration; installed devices require their own
calibration.

A surface area fraction $f_{\mathrm{cov}}$ is not generally the photon
interception probability $f_{\mathrm{hit}}=\sum_j\mathcal G_j$. Even uniform,
isotropic emission inside a rectangular box need not illuminate all walls
uniformly. Under an additional \emph{uniform first-hit wall-flux} approximation,
with no repeated reflections, one may use
\begin{equation}
R_{39}^{\mathrm{TPC}}\simeq
R_{39,\mathrm{decay}}^{\mathrm{TPC}}\,
\overline N_{\gamma,39}\,f_{\mathrm{cov}}\,
\overline\epsilon,\qquad
\overline\epsilon=f_A\epsilon_A+f_L\epsilon_L,
\end{equation}
where $f_A+f_L=1$ are fractions of the instrumented area. With the
\emph{assumed} equal area split, $\overline\epsilon=0.004$. The illustration
is then conveniently written as
\begin{equation}
\boxed{R_{39}^{\mathrm{TPC}}\simeq\SI{1.38e3}{PE/s}
\left(\frac{\overline N_{\gamma,39}}{4400}\right)
\left(\frac{f_{\mathrm{cov}}}{0.25}\right)
\left(\frac{\overline\epsilon}{0.004}\right).}
\label{eq:NDtoy}
\end{equation}
The value 4400 photons/decay is an unvalidated normalization choice, retained
only to show the numerical conversion; it is not a measured yield here.
For eight identical TPCs these assumptions give
$1.10\times10^4$ primary PE/s. The previous 30\% coverage choice would give
$1.65\times10^3$ PE/s/TPC instead. The change reflects the coverage input,
not a change in the underlying decay activity.

Use Eq.~\eqref{eq:ND39exact} with measured panel geometry and response for a
prediction. Equations~\eqref{eq:muPE}--\eqref{eq:CRT} also apply to ND cosmic
light after replacing the volume and detector kernel. For NuMI data, separate
beam-on and beam-off samples and account for beam-correlated light; surface
cosmic commissioning and underground beam operation are different populations.
The 2$\times$2 provides a useful independent test of the activity-to-light
calculation, but its per-TPC toy rate cannot calibrate a per-XA rate without
this response and normalization information.
